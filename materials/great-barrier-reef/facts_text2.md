## Part 1 – A wonder in danger (p. 23, ll. 1–20)
- The Great Barrier Reef is one of the greatest wonders on earth: an underwater world with thousands of reefs and hundreds of islands. (ll. 1–4)
- It stretches for **2,300 kilometres** – about the distance from London to Sicily. (ll. 4–6)
- The area it covers is **larger than the United Kingdom**; you can even see it **from the moon**. (ll. 6–9)
- It is an ecosystem with **more than 350 types of coral** and hundreds of kinds of fish and birds. (ll. 9–11)
- Animals come from thousands of kilometres away; tourists come to see **whales from Antarctica** and **turtles from the Pacific Ocean**. (ll. 11–15)
- The condition of the reef has slowly become worse **since European settlers arrived**. (ll. 16–18)
- **Old photos from 100 years ago or more** show that a lot of the coral has disappeared. (ll. 18–20)

## Part 2 – Climate change and coral bleaching (p. 23, ll. 21–33)
- The **two biggest threats** today: **climate change** and the **higher water temperature** it brings. (ll. 22–24)
- These cause **coral bleaching**, which was **not seen until the 1980s**. (ll. 24–26)
- Coral bleaching happens when the coral is under a lot of **stress** because of changing conditions. (ll. 26–28)
- Warmer water **kills the algae** on the coral, and the coral **loses its colours**. (ll. 28–30)
- When the algae die, the **creatures that need them for food die too**. (ll. 30–32)
- Whole areas of the reef have become **like ghost towns**. (ll. 32–33)

## Part 3 – Tourists (p. 23, ll. 34–42)
- **Two million tourists** visit the reef every year. (l. 35)
- They bring in a lot of **money and many jobs**, but they also **damage** the reef environment. (ll. 36–38)
- Example: **snorkelling platforms** attract **thousands of seabirds**; their **waste** is washed into the sea and **pollutes** the water. (ll. 38–42)

## Part 4 – Ships and oil spills (p. 23, ll. 43–52)
- Many ships travel through the waters; it is also a popular **destination for smaller boats**; sometimes there are accidents. (ll. 44–47)
- One of the worst: **in 2010** a **large Chinese ship** went **outside the official lane**, **hit the reef** and there was a **big oil spill**. (ll. 47–50)
- **Three kilometres of coral** were damaged; it will take **many years** for this part of the reef to **recover**. (ll. 50–52)

## Part 5 – What can the government do? (p. 24, ll. 53–71)
- Saving the reef **costs money** and needs **new ideas**. (ll. 55–56)
- The Australian government has **spent millions of Australian dollars** on help for the reef. (ll. 56–58)
- It has made **stricter laws for ships** in the area. (ll. 58–59)
- A recent development: **underwater robots** help the coral **repair itself**. (ll. 59–61)
- Australia **cannot stop climate change alone**; it has to **work with other countries** – probably the most difficult problem of all. (ll. 62–65)
- The governments of the world have been **struggling for a long time** to find a plan to stop climate change. (ll. 66–68)
- If no solution is found, scientists are afraid the reef **may be dead by 2030**. (ll. 68–70)
- The article ends with a question: Do we care enough about our earth to protect one of its most beautiful places? (ll. 70–71)
